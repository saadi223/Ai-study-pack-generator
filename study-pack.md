## Explanations
### Newton's Second Law Overview
The second law states that the net force acting on an object equals its mass multiplied by its acceleration (F = ma). Force is measured in newtons (N), mass in kilograms (kg), and acceleration in metres per second squared (m/s²).
**Example:** From the notes: a 2 kg object accelerating at 3 m/s² needs a net force of F = 2 kg × 3 m/s² = 6 N.
*Sources: S1*

### Applying the Formula
To solve a force problem, identify the mass (m) and acceleration (a), then multiply them using F = ma. Keep units consistent so the result is in newtons.
**Example:** From the notes: for a 2 kg object accelerating at 3 m/s², F = 6 N.
*Sources: S1*

## Summary
- F = ma links force, mass, and acceleration.
- Force is measured in newtons, mass in kilograms, acceleration in m/s².
- Multiply mass by acceleration to find the net force.
- Example from notes: 2 kg × 3 m/s² = 6 N.

## Key terms
- **Force**: A push or pull on an object, measured in newtons (N).
- **Mass**: The amount of matter in an object, measured in kilograms (kg).
- **Acceleration**: The rate of change of velocity, measured in metres per second squared (m/s²).
- **Newton (N)**: The SI unit of force; 1 N = 1 kg·m/s².
- **Formula F = ma**: Expresses that net force equals mass times acceleration.
